# Module 01: Positioning & Niche

Finding an angle that stands out without boxing yourself into creative misery.

---

## 1. The Trap of "Broad Appeal"

Beginning creators often make content for "everyone interested in tech" or "people who want to grow." This fails because algorithms and human attention reward specificity.

When someone discovers your video, post, or article, their brain asks one question in the first two seconds:
> *"Is this specifically for someone like me?"*

If the answer is vague, they scroll. You do not build authority by being broad. You build authority by solving a concrete problem for a specific group, then gradually widening your aperture as your core audience solidifies.

---

## 2. Skill Stacking vs. The 1% Myth

You do not need to be the single best engineer, designer, or thinker in the world to build an audience. Scott Adams popularized the idea of the "skill stack":
- Being in the top 1% of programmers is extraordinarily difficult.
- Being in the top 20% of programmers, combined with the top 20% of clear writers, combined with basic video editing, puts you in the top 0.1% of that specific intersection.

Your positioning lives at the intersection of two or three distinct domains. For example:
- Python Developer + Narrative Filmmaking = Cinematic code walkthroughs.
- Product Manager + Visual Diagramming = System architecture breakdowns on LinkedIn.
- Data Analyst + Satire/Comedy = Short-form skits on corporate tech life.

---

## 3. Defining Your Audience Avatar

Do not market to a demographic (e.g. "men aged 25-34"). Define an actual person with distinct friction points:
- **Current Struggle**: What tool, task, or concept makes them groan during their workday?
- **Current Hack**: What messy workaround are they using right now to cope?
- **Vocabulary**: What specific slang, jargon, or acronyms do they use when venting in private Slack channels or subreddits?
- **Desired Transformation**: What does relief or success look like for them in 3 months?

When your script or post speaks using their exact vocabulary and cites their specific frustration, viewer retention jumps immediately.

---

## 4. The 3 Content Pillars

To maintain sanity and prevent audience confusion, structure your content into three distinct pillars:

1. **Pillar A: Tactical / Educational (The Foundation)**
   - Actionable step-by-step guides, code walk-throughs, tool reviews, breakdown of hard problems.
   - Purpose: Builds authority, gets bookmarked and shared.

2. **Pillar B: Perspective / Contrarian (The Hook)**
   - Opinions on industry shifts, common advice that is actually terrible, architectural teardowns.
   - Purpose: Sparks discussions in comments, drives follows and shares.

3. **Pillar C: Story / Journey (The Trust Builder)**
   - Personal build-in-public updates, lessons learned from failures, behind-the-scenes hurdles.
   - Purpose: Converts casual viewers into loyal fans who care about *you*, not just the tips.

---

## 5. Positioning Formula

Use this template to test your positioning:

```text
I help [specific avatar] achieve [desirable transformation] without [primary pain point or hated convention].
```

**Weak example**:
*"I make videos about tech and software development."*

**Strong example**:
*"I help junior frontend devs master backend fundamentals without getting buried in DevOps jargon."*
